Essential Question

How do we measure the space a flat shape covers?

Cover the shape with same-size squares, with no gaps and nothing overlapping. Count the squares. That count is the area.

From the past

A floor made of little squares

👀 What am I looking at?

This is a Roman floor. Workers made it by setting thousands of tiny square stone tiles, called tesserae, side by side in rows. Each little square covers the same amount of floor. If you counted all the squares, you would know how much floor the pattern covers. That count is its area.

The Romans measured a floor the way you will measure a shape today: by the squares that cover it.

🎬 Cover It, Count It
Learn

What area is

Area is how much flat space a shape covers. We measure it in square units. To find the area, cover the shape with same-size squares, leave no gaps, let none overlap, and count the squares. A shape with 3 rows and 4 columns of squares is covered by 12 square units.

Learn

Half squares for a slanted edge

Some shapes have a slanted edge. There, a square is cut along the slant into two triangle halves. Two half tiles make one whole square unit, so the area is still a whole number.

The Math Crew

Four ways to stay on track

I leave no gaps and let no tiles overlap. One tile fills each square.
Every tile has to be the same size, or the count isn't fair.
I touch each square as I count, so I never skip one or count it twice.
At a slanted edge, I remember two half tiles make one whole unit.
JOB 2

Your Turn: Lay the Tiles

🔨 Do

The worker starts a new floor. Cover it with square tiles, with no gaps and no overlaps, to find its area.

👀 What am I looking at?

An empty floor shape, drawn as cream squares. Tap an empty square to lay a tile on it. Tap a tile again to lift it off. You can also drag the tile from the tray. Cover every square.

Cover every square

Lay a tile on each empty square until the whole shape is covered. Leave no gaps, and don't stack two tiles on one square.

Find the area

You covered the floor with no gaps and no overlaps. Its area is [[n:15~Count the tiles: 3 rows with 5 in each row.]] square units.

JOB 3

The Stepped Floor

🔨 Do

This floor is not a plain rectangle. It has a step cut out of one corner. You can still find its area by covering it.

👀 What am I looking at?

A floor shape with a corner missing. Tap each empty square to lay a tile, the same as before. Cover every square, even though the shape is not a rectangle.

Cover the stepped floor

Lay a tile on every empty square. The missing corner is not part of the floor, so leave it empty.

Count the area

The stepped floor has an area of [[n:11~Count the tiles you laid. The missing corner is not counted.]] square units.

A shape does not have to be a rectangle to have an area. You cover it with the same square units and count them, just the same.

JOB 4

Count Without Slipping

🔨 Do

This panel is already tiled. Count the square tiles carefully, so you don't skip one or count one twice.

👀 What am I looking at?

A finished panel, already covered with tiles. Tap each tile to count it. The number shows up on the tile you tapped, so you can see which ones are left.

Count every tile

Tap each tile once, in any order you like, until every tile has a number.

Give the area

The panel's area is [[n:13~The last number you tapped is the total.]] square units.

JOB 5

Hazel's Placemat

🔨 Do · ✏️ Write

Hazel covered a placemat with paper squares to find its area. She left gaps between some squares and let others overlap, then counted the squares she saw.

👀 What am I looking at?

First, Hazel's try: her squares have gaps between some and overlaps on others, so her count can't be the true area. Then you get the same placemat, empty. Lay the tiles the right way, with no gaps and no overlaps, and count.

Tile the placemat the right way

Lay a tile on every square, with no gaps and no overlaps. One tile fills each square.

The true area

Covered the right way, the placemat's true area is [[n:12~Count your tiles: 2 rows of 6, with no gaps and no overlaps.]] square units.

Help Hazel

Remember to tell: no gapsno overlapssame-size squares

Gaps leave floor uncovered, and overlaps count the same floor twice. Only same-size squares with no gaps and no overlaps give the true area.

JOB 6

On Your Own: The Slanted Edge

🔨 Do · ✏️ Write

This mosaic piece has a slanted top edge. The worker fills the straight part with whole tiles and the slanted part with half tiles.

👀 What am I looking at?

A shape with whole squares along the bottom and triangle half squares along the slanted top. Tap each whole square to lay a whole tile, and tap each triangle to lay a half tile. Cover the whole piece.

Lay the whole tiles and the half tiles

Tap each whole square to fill it, and tap each triangle to fill it with a half tile. Cover every part of the piece.

Add up the area

The piece has 8 whole tiles and 4 half tiles. Two halves make one whole, so 4 half tiles make [[n:2~Pair the half tiles: 4 halves make 2 wholes.]] whole units. The area is [[n:10~8 whole units and 2 more from the halves.]] square units.
